Setup guide

Getting started

You'll need OBS Studio 28 or newer on the same computer, and a StreamerSongList or StreamElements account. Setup is two steps: connect your songs, then put BubbleFacts on your stream. A third step, about your music, is optional.

  1. Install the app
  2. Connect your songs
  3. Put BubbleFacts on your stream
  4. Tell us about your music (optional)
  5. Check that it works
  6. While you stream

Also on this page: Use StreamElements instead, Custom facts, If a fact is wrong and Optional: use an online AI instead.

Stuck? Jump to If something goes wrong.

1

Install the app

Download the version for your computer from the download page (not sure which Mac you have? Here's how to check), then follow the steps for your system.

About the security warnings

The beta isn't signed yet (signing is a paid registration with Apple and Microsoft), so your computer warns you the first time you open BubbleFacts. The steps below show what to click.

Mac

  1. Open the BubbleFacts .dmg file from your Downloads folder.
  2. Drag BubbleFacts into the Applications folder.
  3. Open BubbleFacts from your Applications folder. On macOS 15 (Sequoia) and newer, your Mac says "BubbleFacts" Not Opened. Click Done.
  4. Open System Settings, then Privacy & Security. Scroll down and click Open Anyway next to the note about BubbleFacts, enter your Mac password, and click Open Anyway once more.

On older Macs: right-click BubbleFacts (or Control-click), choose Open, then click Open again. You only need to do this once.

Windows

  1. Open the BubbleFacts .exe file from your Downloads folder. If your browser says the file isn't commonly downloaded: in Microsoft Edge, point at the download, click …, then Keep, Show more and Keep anyway.
  2. Windows may show a blue box saying Windows protected your PC. Click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. The installer sets up BubbleFacts just for you. It doesn't need an administrator password.

Linux

Ubuntu, Debian and similar: use the .deb file (its name ends in _amd64.deb).

  1. Double-click the BubbleFacts .deb file in your Downloads folder.
  2. If it opens in an archive viewer instead, close that. Right-click the file, choose Open With, then App Center (Ubuntu 24.04 and newer) or Software Install.
  3. Click Install and type your computer's password when asked.

Other Linux: use the AppImage.

  1. Right-click the BubbleFacts .AppImage file and choose Properties.
  2. Turn on Allow executing file as program (the wording varies).
  3. Double-click the file to open it.

On Ubuntu 24.04 and newer, an AppImage may not open without an extra system part. If that happens, use the .deb instead.

2

Connect your songs

The first time you open BubbleFacts, a short setup starts. Its first step connects the app to your song requests, so it can see which song you're playing. It asks Where do your song requests come from? Leave StreamerSongList picked, or see StreamElements below.

  1. Click the big Sign in with StreamerSongList button. Your web browser opens.
  2. Sign in with the Twitch account you use on StreamerSongList.
  3. Go back to BubbleFacts. Your channel is filled in for you, and setup moves on.
Having trouble signing in?

You can connect with a token instead. Open Having trouble signing in? under the sign-in button. It asks for two things.

  1. Your channel name. Type your StreamerSongList channel name.
  2. Your Streamer Access Token. To get it:
    1. In your web browser, sign in at streamersonglist.com.
    2. Go to Settings, then Access.
    3. Create a Streamer Access Token and copy it.
    4. Back in BubbleFacts, paste it in.
  3. Click Connect. If it doesn't work, check the channel name, or create a new token and paste it again.

Treat a token like a password. Keep it off your stream and out of screenshots.

Use StreamElements instead

Do viewers request songs through StreamElements' Media Request player? Connect that instead. StreamElements has no sign-in button for apps like this, so you paste a token.

  1. Under Where do your song requests come from?, choose StreamElements.
  2. In your web browser, open your StreamElements dashboard. (The Open StreamElements link in the app takes you there.)
  3. Go to Account, then Channels, and click Show secrets.
  4. Copy the JWT token (not the Overlay token).
  5. Back in BubbleFacts, paste the token. Your channel name is optional: the token tells BubbleFacts which channel it is.
  6. Click Connect. BubbleFacts checks the token, then setup moves on.

The JWT token works like a password. Keep it off your stream and out of screenshots.

StreamElements only knows each request's YouTube video title, such as "Artist - Song (Official Video)". BubbleFacts reads the artist and song out of it, so clearly titled videos get the best facts. There are no live learns with StreamElements, so that setting is hidden.

To switch later, or to paste a new token, open Settings, then Your song list, and click Save.

Your sign-in stays private

BubbleFacts stores your sign-in, or your token, encrypted on your computer (on Linux, that needs a keyring).

BubbleFacts gets ready in the background

While you set up, BubbleFacts downloads its built-in fact writer, once (about 2 GB). You don't wait for it: until it's ready, songs get facts from music databases and your own facts. The dashboard shows how far along it is.

3

Put BubbleFacts on your stream

The second step puts BubbleFacts into OBS, on top of your scene. You do this once. BubbleFacts needs to run on the same computer as OBS.

  1. Open OBS Studio.
  2. Choose the scene you normally stream from.
  3. In BubbleFacts, drag the tile that says Drag this into your Sources list onto the Sources list in OBS.

As soon as OBS loads it, BubbleFacts puts a test bubble on your stream and says It's on your stream! OBS names the new source "BubbleFacts" and sizes it to fit your canvas.

Not ready yet? Click I'll add it later. The app's dashboard has a Drag into OBS tile for when you are.

Nothing happens when you drop it on Windows?

OBS may be running as administrator. Close OBS and open it the usual way, or add it by hand.

Add it by hand
  1. In OBS, under Sources, click + and choose Browser. Name it "BubbleFacts" and click OK.
  2. In BubbleFacts, open Add it by hand and click Copy next to the location of BubbleFacts.html. (After setup, use Copy the OBS file path under Help and troubleshooting on the dashboard.)
  3. In OBS, check Local file. Click Browse and choose the file at the location you copied. (You can paste the location into the file window: on a Mac press Cmd+Shift+G first; on Windows paste it into the address bar.)
  4. Set Width and Height to your canvas size. That's usually:
    Width 1920
    Height 1080
    Not sure? In OBS, open Settings, then Video, and look at Base (Canvas) Resolution.
  5. Click OK.

Use Local file, not a URL. It doesn't matter whether you open OBS or BubbleFacts first: the overlay keeps trying until the app is running.

Stream from more than one scene?

Add BubbleFacts to just one scene, for example a scene called "BubbleFacts overlay". Then, in each of your other scenes, click + under Sources, choose Scene, and pick "BubbleFacts overlay". That's easier than adding BubbleFacts six times, and any change happens everywhere at once.

4

Tell us about your music (optional)

Setup is done once the test bubble shows: click Start using BubbleFacts. This part is optional. Open it from the link under the OBS step, or any time in the app's Settings.

I play my own compositions

This is off to start with. Check it if you play your own pieces. Songs tagged "Originals" in StreamerSongList, or with you as the artist, then get facts from your song list instead of a Wikipedia lookup: how often you've played it and who requested it. Your name is already on the request, so it doesn't say you wrote it.

When it's checked, an optional box appears: About your compositions. Type facts about your pieces there, one per line. They show when you play your originals.

I do live learns

This is on to start with, and only for StreamerSongList. When someone requests a song that isn't on your list, a LIVE LEARN banner shows while you learn it. Facts still show when BubbleFacts can find the song, or when you've added facts for it; it never fills in with custom facts. If you uncheck it, those requests are treated like any other song.

Custom facts

When a song has no Wikipedia article, the AI isn't used. BubbleFacts looks the song up on Wikidata, then MusicBrainz, two free music databases, and fills in plain sentences such as the year and the album.

If neither has the song, the app shows your custom facts: short facts you write yourself. With none, that song gets no bubbles rather than filler, which suits request channels.

Custom facts can show during any song no source knows, so write facts that fit any song, like something about you or your instrument. For a fact about one song, artist or game, start the line with its name in brackets, like [Song of Storms] It plays in a windmill. or [Chopin] …. It then shows only for that, ahead of other facts. You can also add facts while the song plays.

Only add facts you've checked

Your facts go on stream exactly as you write them. Nothing checks them for you.

Add or change your facts later

  1. In the app, open Settings, then Custom facts.
  2. In Your own facts, type one fact per line.
  3. Optional: in About your own compositions, add facts about your original pieces. These show when you play one of your originals.

Five or more custom facts keep the same ones from repeating song after song.

Advanced in Settings also has a few example packs (video game music, classical, film and TV, pop and rock, piano, general music). They're off unless you check them, they aren't updated, and they won't know your songs, so your own facts are better.

5

Check that it works

  1. Make sure BubbleFacts is open.
  2. Play a song from your StreamerSongList queue, or your StreamElements song requests.
  3. A gold NOW PLAYING banner appears at the bottom of your OBS preview. A few seconds later, the first fact bubble pops up.

The dashboard shows three lights: Songs, Facts and Stream. To check the overlay any time, click Show a test bubble.

Few or no facts at first? BubbleFacts may still be getting ready. The dashboard's Facts light shows how far along it is.

Seeing a small red dot in the bottom-right corner instead? The overlay can't reach the app. See If something goes wrong.

6

While you stream

  • Need the bubbles gone right now? Click Pause bubbles on the dashboard, or in the BubbleFacts menu in your menu bar (Mac) or system tray (Windows and Linux). Turn them back on the same way.
  • Closing the window doesn't stop it. BubbleFacts keeps running in your menu bar (Mac) or system tray (Windows and Linux), with a status light. To stop it, choose Quit from that menu.
  • It fixes itself. If something inside the app gets stuck, it restarts that part on its own, so you can keep playing.
  • Wrong fact? Click Wrong next to it on the dashboard. See If a fact is wrong.
  • Something else wrong? Open Help and troubleshooting and click Report a problem. A report opens on GitHub in your browser for you to read before you send it. You need a free GitHub account. See how reports work.
  • New versions. When one is out, the app tells you. Click Update now, then Install now between songs. Your settings are kept. See Updating and what's new.
  • Bubble size. The bubbles grow and shrink with the source, so they look the same on a 720p, 1080p or 4K canvas. If viewers on phones find them small, open Settings, then Bubbles, and set Bubble size to Large or Larger.
  • Add your own facts. Songs no source knows get your custom facts. See how to add them, or add facts for one song.

If a fact is wrong

The dashboard's On stream now list shows the facts for the song you're playing. Each one has a Wrong button.

  1. Click Wrong next to the fact. It comes off your stream right away, even if its bubble is showing.
  2. BubbleFacts never uses that fact's source for that song again: the Wikipedia article, or Wikidata and MusicBrainz. It remembers this after you quit and reopen the app. A wrong fact almost always means it found the wrong source. Clicked by mistake? Click Undo. (Your own facts are only taken off for now: change them in Settings, or with Add facts for this song.)
  3. Optional: click Report it (opens GitHub). A report opens on GitHub in your browser, with the song and the fact filled in, for you to read before you send it. You need a free GitHub account.

Reports of wrong facts are the most useful kind. They show where the fact check needs to be stricter.

Add facts for one song

Some songs aren't on Wikipedia or in any music database: another streamer's original, a friend's band, a song you wrote together. You can give a song its own facts.

  1. While the song plays, click Add facts for this song under On stream now. Getting ready before a show? Click Preparing a different song? and type the title and the artist or game as they'll appear on the request.
  2. Optional: under Who wrote it?, type the songwriter, but only if you're sure. BubbleFacts never guesses who wrote a song.
  3. Optional: add their link, like their Twitch page. It always gets a bubble, with their name.
  4. Type your facts, one per line, and click Save and show.

Every song you've done this for is listed in Settings, under Songs with their own facts, with Edit and Remove.

If the song is still playing, they show right away. Either way, they show exactly as you wrote them every time that song plays. On the dashboard, each fact says where it came from, so you can tell your own from Wikipedia's. Click a Wikipedia source to see the sentence the fact was written from, and to open the article.

Optional: use an online AI instead

You don't need to choose an AI: by default, the facts are written on your own computer. On an older computer, or one that's already busy while you stream, an online service can write them instead.

In the app, open Settings, then Advanced, then Who writes the facts. You can switch back any time.

Online, with Groq (free key) Free, with a daily limit A free Groq account and an API key from console.groq.com/keys. Good for older computers, or if yours is already busy while you stream.
Online, with Anthropic Paid, but cheap: a fraction of a cent per song An Anthropic account and an API key from platform.claude.com/settings/keys.
My own Ollama (advanced) Free For people who already use Ollama, installed with its normal installer from ollama.com. Point BubbleFacts at it. The app downloads the Ollama model for you the first time.

An API key is a long password that lets the app use an online service for you. Copy it from the service's website and paste it into BubbleFacts. It's stored encrypted on your computer (on Linux, that needs a keyring).

If the built-in AI can't run on your computer

This can happen on older processors. The app tries a simpler, processor-only mode on its own. If that doesn't work either, it tells you, and its Switch to Groq button opens Settings so you can choose Online, with Groq (free key).

Fewer bubbles than expected Some captions failed the fact check That's normal for songs with short articles. It means the check is working.

Still stuck? In the app, open Help and troubleshooting and click Report a problem, or ask on Discord. The help page lists every option.
