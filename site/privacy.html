Privacy

Your data, in plain words

The short version: BubbleFacts runs on your computer. It talks to your song request service (StreamerSongList or StreamElements), to Wikipedia, Wikidata and MusicBrainz to look songs up, and to an online AI only if you choose one. Nothing is sent to this project.

  • Everything runs on your computer

    The app, the overlay and the built-in AI all run on your own computer. There's no BubbleFacts account and no BubbleFacts server.

  • Your settings stay on your computer

    Your settings are saved in the app's folder on your computer, along with the facts you add for particular songs the sources you've marked Wrong, and the facts your current stream has shown (so a restart doesn't repeat them). They stay on your computer. A backup you make with Back up… holds your settings and facts, never your sign-in or keys, and goes only where you save it. Your StreamerSongList sign-in (or token), your StreamElements JWT token, your Twitch connection if you made one, and any API keys are encrypted using your system's keychain, the same secure storage your computer uses for its own passwords. On Linux that needs a keyring such as GNOME Keyring or KWallet; without one, they're saved without real encryption, and the app warns you.

  • StreamerSongList

    The app reads your request queue from StreamerSongList, using your sign-in (or your token, if you use one), so it knows which song you're playing. You sign in on StreamerSongList's own website, in your web browser, so BubbleFacts never sees your Twitch password.

  • StreamElements

    If you use StreamElements instead, the app uses your JWT token and channel name to ask StreamElements which song request is playing, and to get told when it changes. You copy the token from your StreamElements dashboard yourself.

  • Wikipedia, Wikidata and MusicBrainz

    Song titles, and the artist or game name, are sent to Wikipedia to look up articles about the song. When Wikipedia has no article, the same names are sent to Wikidata, then MusicBrainz, two public music databases.

  • Twitch (only if you connect it)

    If you click Connect Twitch, you approve BubbleFacts on Twitch's own site with a short code, and it asks for no permissions. When you click Fill from their Twitch About, BubbleFacts asks Twitch for that one channel's public About text. Nothing about you or your viewers is read. Disconnect ends the connection on Twitch too.

  • YouTube (StreamElements only)

    StreamElements song requests are YouTube videos. Some are uploads YouTube makes automatically (their channel names end in "- Topic"). For those only, when your copy of the app has a YouTube key built in, it sends the video ID to Google's YouTube Data API to read the exact artist and song. Nothing else about you or your stream is sent. Google's privacy policy applies. Otherwise, the app only reads the video's title.

  • The AI

    With the built-in AI, the default, captions are written on your computer. The AI model is downloaded once, from Hugging Face, a site that hosts AI models. After that, nothing is sent anywhere to write captions.

    If you choose an online AI (Groq or Anthropic), only the song title, artist and the Wikipedia article text are sent to that service to write the captions. Nothing about you or your channel is. Their own privacy policies apply. If you choose your own Ollama, they go to the Ollama address you set.

  • Nothing is sent to the project

    No analytics, no tracking, no usage counts. The app does check GitHub for new versions when it starts, which is how it can tell you an update is out. If you click Update now, it downloads the new version from GitHub, checks it against the release's checksum list, and installs it when you click Install now.

  • Problem reports only happen when you click

    A report is only created when you click Report it (opens GitHub) after marking a fact Wrong, or Report a problem. It opens GitHub in your browser with the report already filled in, so GitHub receives those details when the page opens: things like the song, the fact, the app version, your system and recent log lines. Your token is removed. Clicking Wrong on its own sends nothing: BubbleFacts just remembers it on your computer.

    Nothing is posted until you click Create. If you do, it's posted on GitHub publicly, under your own GitHub account, where anyone can see it. Edit out anything you don't want to share before you submit.

  • This website

    This site has no cookies, analytics or trackers. To show the newest version and its download links, the download buttons ask GitHub's public API about the latest release. GitHub sees that request like any visit to GitHub. Nothing else is loaded from other companies. Like any website, the web host may keep basic server logs, such as IP addresses, for security.

Questions? Ask on Discord. The app is open source, so anyone can check what it does on GitHub.
